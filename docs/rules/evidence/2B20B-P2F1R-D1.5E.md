## Required evidence answers

### 1. Why artifact authority and evidence authority must remain separate

P and E answer different questions and exist at different times.

- P is artifact authority: its tree contains the accepted D1.5 product/profile result, including the accepted 1712 baseline/profile, static selector state, and any retained stable verifier behavior. Consumers of product behavior must obtain those bytes from P.
- E is evidence authority: it can exist only after P exists, and may archive a manifest and traceability/audit facts that bind the already-existing P and direct artifact hashes. E supports inspection of what P was; it is not a second product/profile source.

Collapsing the two authorities requires a commit to contain evidence derived from its own not-yet-existing object ID, or makes later evidence bytes silently become product authority. Separating them preserves temporal reachability and prevents provenance records from changing or reinterpreting the accepted artifact. A digest proves exact bytes only; it does not itself prove BOTC correctness, acceptance, coverage quality, game truth, or independent review.

This separation is also consistent with ADR-001's authority boundary: canonical game state comes from the domain event log; snapshots are rebuildable caches; audit and infrastructure events do not rebuild game state; historical rule-version events cannot be silently reinterpreted. D1.5E evidence is repository governance evidence, never canonical game or product state.

### 2. Why Git/content-addressed self-reference is unacceptable

A Git commit object ID is computed from commit bytes that include its tree and parent. If a file in that tree contains the commit's supposed object ID, inserting or changing that value changes the tree and therefore changes the commit object ID. The embedded value then names different bytes. The same dependency failure occurs when P must contain the SHA of a manifest whose content includes P's exact object ID: P must exist before the manifest can be computed, but changing P afterward produces P-prime and invalidates the manifest's binding.

A hash-of-hash, manifest-of-manifest, evidence self-digest, recursive snapshot, reserved future ID, or amend/writeback protocol does not cure this ordering defect. It adds another content-addressed dependency or rewrites the object being attested. No self-referential value may be treated as stable authority, and no history rewrite may be used to manufacture one.

### 3. Why E does not need and must not contain its own SHA

E's legitimate inputs already exist before E is created: P's exact object ID, the P-bound manifest bytes, direct artifact SHA-256 values, and traceability/audit facts about P. Git computes E's identity only after E's parent and tree are fixed. Therefore E can record P and its artifacts without recording, predicting, deriving, reserving, or hashing E itself.

E's own SHA is unnecessary because Git supplies E's identity externally after the commit exists. Including that identity in E would change E's tree, change E's computed identity, and recreate the same recursive dependency. An E self-SHA, an E-bound manifest inside E, or an internal assertion of E's future review status is prohibited. E must also omit future CI records, PR URLs, review-comment URLs, and external verdicts that do not exist when E is committed.

### 4. Why a fresh review can externally bind E's SHA

After E exists, an independent reviewer can observe E's actual Git object ID, verify that E is the expected direct single-parent child of P, inspect E's exact tree, re-read the P-bound manifest and direct artifact hashes, and issue a complete report naming `reviewedHead=<actual E SHA>`. The exact-E binding is stored outside E, including the required GitHub audit comments when the later workflow reaches that stage. Nothing is written back into E.

This is temporally reachable because every referenced object already exists when the reviewer acts. It is also fail-closed: any commit after a passing exact-E review changes HEAD and invalidates that review and both audit comments, requiring fresh CI and a new complete review for the new HEAD. The external review proves what exact repository head was reviewed; it does not convert E into product/profile authority or BOTC rule authority.

## Authority boundary

The bounded authority chain is `S -> P -> E -> external exact-E review`, with no reverse or self-referential edge.

1. `SOURCE_HEAD_S` is the source/producer-input boundary. S contains no future P or E object ID.
2. `PROFILE_CHILD_P` is the product/profile artifact authority. P may bind S and already-existing pre-P inputs. P contains no P self-ID, no E ID, and no SHA of a P-bound manifest that can exist only after P.
3. `EVIDENCE_HEAD_E` is a docs/evidence-only repository evidence anchor. E may record exact P, the P-bound manifest bytes, direct artifact hashes, and traceability/audit facts. E is authoritative only for the evidence chain. It cannot modify or reinterpret P, cannot be an artifact/profile source, and cannot contain its own object ID.
4. Fresh independent review is external final-review authority. It names actual E after E exists. A later commit invalidates the exact-E review.
5. A separately authorized future D2 may start from E as repository ancestry and evidence custody, but its product/profile input remains P and the artifacts identified from P. D2 must not treat E as runtime, selector, baseline, coverage-profile, BOTC-rule, or gameplay authority.

The minimal reachability invariants supported by this evidence are:

- `parent(P)=S` and `parent(E)=P`, each with exactly one parent;
- no amendment or rewrite of S or P to backfill a later identity;
- P contains no E SHA and no E-derived value;
- E contains no E SHA and no E-derived self-digest;
- P-bound evidence is created only after P exists and is archived only in E or externally;
- E changes evidence documentation only and cannot change the artifact it attests;
- exact-E review occurs only after E exists and remains external to E.
