## abilityRules
- Once per game, at night, the Seamstress may choose exactly two players other than themself.
- The Seamstress learns whether the two chosen players currently have the same alignment.
- The two targets may be alive or dead and may include Travellers.
- The Seamstress may decline to use the ability on a night.
- After choosing and receiving information, the once-per-game ability is spent.
C1 must not modify, reinterpret, or claim to newly implement any of these rules.

## firstNightOrder
At the fixed official nightsheet revision: `seamstress` is zero-based index `61`, displayed position `62` of `80`; immediately after `dreamer`; immediately before `steward`. C1 makes no night-order change.

## otherNightOrder
At the fixed official nightsheet revision: `seamstress` is zero-based index `82`, displayed position `83` of `99`; immediately after `oracle`; immediately before `juggler`. The official ability is available each night until spent. C1 makes no other-night implementation claim.

## interactions
- The Seamstress compares alignment, not character type or default character alignment.
- Character change does not inherently change alignment. The official example where the Pit-Hag changes the Mathematician into a Witch while the player remains good confirms actual alignments.
- C1 introduces no new role interaction.
- `representedImpairments` is an internal accepted-event audit collection. Its cardinality is not defined by BOTC rules and must not be presented as a new rule claim.
- Supporting a non-empty variable-length `representedImpairments` collection is solely an accepted schema-compatibility obligation. It neither asserts that a Seamstress must have multiple impairments nor places an official maximum on impairments or provenance records.

## drunkennessRules
- A drunk player has no ability.
- The Storyteller behaves as though the player has the ability and does not tell the player they are drunk.
- Information given to a drunk information role may be true or false.
- Using a once-per-game ability while drunk consumes that use.
- Drunkenness belongs to the player and persists through character change unless the effect causing it ends.
- Drunkenness and poisoning do not cancel each other.
C1 does not implement or alter these semantics.

## poisoningRules
- Poisoning has the same no-ability effect as drunkenness.
- A poisoned player is not told that they are poisoned.
- Information given to a poisoned information role may be true or false.
- Using a once-per-game ability while poisoned consumes that use.
- Poisoning belongs to the player and persists through character change unless its source effect ends.
- A player may simultaneously be drunk and poisoned.
These rules do not define an event-payload provenance-array shape or cardinality. C1’s non-empty variable-array compatibility is not a claim that official rules require one, two, or any particular number of provenance records.

## VortoxRules
The official Vortox source establishes that information yielded by Townsfolk abilities must be false while the Vortox ability is effective, including when the receiving Townsfolk is drunk or poisoned. C1 preserves this rule boundary but does not implement, expand, or evaluate Vortox behavior.

## characterChangeRules
- Character and alignment are independent states.
- Changing character does not automatically change alignment.
- Drunkenness and poisoning attach to the player and are not cleared merely by changing character.
- C1 performs no character change and validates no character-change legality.

## alignmentChangeRules
- The Seamstress answers whether the two selected players have the same current alignment.
- Character token type or default alignment cannot substitute for current player alignment.
- C1 performs no alignment change and validates no alignment-change legality.

## storytellerDiscretion
- For a sober and healthy Seamstress without an effective false-information constraint, the same-alignment answer follows actual alignments.
- For a drunk or poisoned Seamstress, the Storyteller may provide true or false information; the player is not told of the impairment.
- With an effective Vortox, Townsfolk ability information must be false, including if the Townsfolk is drunk or poisoned.
- C1 introduces no Storyteller selection policy or deterministic information policy.
